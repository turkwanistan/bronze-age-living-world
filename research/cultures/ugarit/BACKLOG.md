# Demand-driven Ugarit research backlog

Only research items that can change the first 6–10-household slice are prioritized.

## P0 — blocks historically specific mechanics

1. **Household composition and property control** — distinguish urban joint-family proposals, smaller households, widow/elder property control, dependents, servants/bound labor, and household infrastructure inequality. Need source-specific limits rather than a universal patrilocal template.
2. **Ordinary property/debt/obligation practice at Ugarit** — what is directly attested for household loans, pledges, repayment, witnesses, merchant credit, and dispute escalation; separate palace/elite archives from common household inference.
3. **Seasonal labor and subsistence around c. 1350 BCE** — Levantine dry-farming work windows, cereal/vine/olive/herd interactions, storage risks, and labor conflicts. Seek ranges, not false daily precision.
4. **Household ritual and access to specialists** — what ordinary households plausibly did for illness, birth, crops, livestock, weather, vows/divination; material costs and when temple/local shrine/specialist access matters.
5. **Water/access inequality** — private wells and household infrastructure; what can be safely turned into access constraints versus archaeological observation only.
6. **Attested personal-name pool by date/social context** — enough names for simulated ordinary people without implying they are known historical individuals.

## P1 — needed by first cognition scenes

7. **Reputation, reciprocity, and patronage at Ugarit** — Ugarit-specific evidence versus broader Near Eastern analogies.
8. **Women’s property/legal/social agency outside the royal household** — identify what is Ugarit-specific, what is regional comparison, and what remains uncertain.
9. **Status/dependence categories** — avoid binary free/slave; establish which categories are attested and what rights/constraints can be modeled.
10. **Neighborhood dispute resolution and institutional access** — household negotiation, elders/patrons, palace officials, temple/oath mechanisms, formal adjudication.
11. **Merchant–scribe–palace interfaces** — who controls records, credit, storage, requisitions, and information in the first small world.
12. **Language proficiency and oral/written information paths** — which first-slice roles plausibly use Ugaritic/Akkadian and when a scribe/interpreter is required.
