# Historical Fidelity Doctrine

## Evidence grades

- **A — direct/contemporaneous:** texts, contracts, archaeology, securely contextualized material, human remains, residues, isotopes, aDNA.
- **B — strong synthesis:** convergent specialist scholarship.
- **C — plausible reconstruction:** comparative anthropology constrained by local evidence.
- **D — speculative:** serious hypothesis with substantial ambiguity.

Grade D is never a default behavioral rule.

## Three uncertainties

These must never share one confidence field:

1. **historical/model uncertainty** — what modern researchers do not know;
2. **in-world epistemic uncertainty** — what a simulated person does not know;
3. **runtime stochastic uncertainty** — what has not yet been sampled in this run.

## Anti-stereotype rule

Never derive a person’s dispositions from `culture = Ugaritic` or any civilization label. Culture may determine institutional access, concepts, expected obligations, sanctions, ritual repertoire, language opportunities, property constraints, and social costs. Individuals vary within those structures.

## Archive-bias rule

Palace archives, elite tombs, letters, legal records, destruction layers, and biological evidence illuminate different slices of society. Absence from one source class is not automatically social absence.

## Naming rule

Text-rich Ugarit permits use of securely attested names, but a fixture character using an attested name is a **simulated identity**, not the historical individual, unless the scenario explicitly models that historical person. Unverified ordinary names remain a research backlog item.

## Quantification rule

Do not invent precise historical household consumption, wage, fertility, disease, or household-size constants. The current fixture uses abstract resource units strictly to exercise causal mechanics. Historical calibration must replace or bound them before interpretation of quantitative outputs.

## Ugarit V0.1 constraints inherited from the supplied encyclopedia

- multilingual scribal environment;
- households and household infrastructure can be unequal;
- maritime commerce, merchants, palace interests, and agriculture overlap;
- household cult concerns include health, birth, prosperity, weather, crops, and livestock;
- women’s agency must not be erased by a generic patriarchal label;
- information can be delayed, distorted, and unevenly distributed;
- institutions have uneven reach and access.

## Research mode vs character mode

Research mode may use the web and outside scholarship. Character cognition mode may use only the compiled packet and project rules. A decision that relies on a factual premise absent from the packet is invalid even if the model happens to know it.
